# Dr. Priya Mehta — Chief Data and AI Officer

> AI outcomes + responsible use

## Notes
Main Topic: The central theme is addressing critical data governance and lineage issues in banking to comply with regulations, mitigate financial losses, and enable future AI-driven self-service capabilities.
Key Discussion Points:
Regulatory Compliance Challenges:
Core Problem: The bank faces significant hurdles with regulations (VCBS, FCA) due to a lack of data provenance and lineage, hindering the deployment and use of critical financial models.
Specific Example (SME Credit Risk Model): A machine learning credit calculation model for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) is suspended because the bank cannot prove the data lineage and quality used for its training. This forces manual processing of £340 million in SME credits.
Financial Impact of Non-Compliance:
P&L Impact: The inability to use the automated model results in an estimated P&L impact of £10.3 million (derived from 18 basis points on the £340 million portfolio) due to reduced precision and potential opportunities lost.
Operational Costs: An additional £4.2 million is spent annually on manual labour (internal staff and partners like Accenture) to perform credit reviews that the suspended model would otherwise handle.
Strategic Vision for AI and Data:
Future Goals: Beyond fixing immediate regulatory issues, the bank aims for a broader AI strategy: opening the data platform to all employees, enabling natural language queries for risk modelers and the CFO to understand model performance and data insights.
Technology Exploration: The bank is currently evaluating tools like Snowflake Intelligence (now Snowflake Cortex) and Sigma for these self-service AI capabilities.
Vendor's Proposed Solutions and Differentiators:
Addressing Lineage and Governance: The vendor asserts their platform can easily solve data lineage and provenance issues through out-of-the-box governance capabilities, which is a foundational aspect of their offering.
Proven Track Record: They highlight experience with numerous global banks (G2K, e.g., JP Morgan) in similar situations.
Key Differentiators:
Speed: Ability to meet short deadlines (e.g., two quarters) due to an easy-to-use platform with minimal configuration.
Out-of-the-Box Functionality: Offers pre-built capabilities, reducing the need for custom development.
Expertise: Leverages internal expert teams with prior banking experience, ensuring a shorter learning curve and faster implementation for regulatory compliance.
AI/Self-Service Tools: Clarification that Snowflake Intelligence has been rebranded to Snowflake Cortex, enabling users to ask questions in plain English, which is useful for non-technical executives. They also explain Sigma's role for users who prefer an Excel-like interface, noting both can leverage Snowflake compute.
Knowledge Graphs: The vendor confirms they are working on Knowledge Graphs to consolidate enterprise-wide "tribal knowledge" and documentation, enhancing data understanding and trust.
Further Opportunities and Requirements:
Fraud Analysis/AML: The banking professional expresses interest in fraud analysis and anti-money laundering (AML) as other areas with potential P&L impact, requesting collaboration with internal teams like Model Risk Engineering.
Value Proposition: Emphasises the need for the vendor to clearly articulate their unique value proposition, focusing on concrete cost-benefit analyses over generic claims about lineage or data accessibility.
